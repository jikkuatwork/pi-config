# Art Direction and Originality

## Extract visual grammar, not identity

When the user supplies a reference, inspect the complete available evidence before coding: desktop and mobile captures, motion, section order, copy, assets, and interactions. Separate two lists.

### Reusable grammar

- density and negative-space rhythm;
- hierarchy and type contrast;
- grid and alignment behavior;
- palette relationships rather than sampled swatches;
- media treatment, crop logic, and depth;
- chapter pacing and contrast changes;
- interaction purpose and motion character.

### Identity-bearing elements to replace

- brand name, wordmark, slogans, distinctive copy, and URLs;
- people, products, screenshots, logos, customers, and metrics;
- proprietary illustrations, textures, icons, and media;
- distinctive motif arrangement or near-identical section sequence;
- source-specific animation choreography when the combination itself is recognizable.

Common grammar such as large type, a dark background, a two-column hero, or a simple fade is not exclusive. Risk rises when several distinctive elements are preserved together.

## Pick one visual lane

Use these as starting directions, not templates.

### Warm technical paper

Use warm off-white surfaces inside a darker frame, strict rules and corner details, sans display type, mono metadata, and one crisp signal color. The result should feel engineered but tactile. Keep texture faint and internal geometry precise.

Best for: tools, creative systems, research, product narratives, and technical showcases.

Avoid: distressed vintage paper, rounded consumer softness, many accent colors, or arbitrary diagrams.

### Editorial chapters

Lead with work or proof, then alternate chapters with deliberate changes in scale or tone. Pair decisive display typography with readable body copy and sparse utility labels. Reserve full-bleed moments for the strongest evidence.

Best for: studios, portfolios, campaigns, cultural projects, and case studies.

Avoid: explaining the organization before showing the work, identical cards for every section, or actions hidden until hover.

### Framed precision

Expose a consistent parent grid through hairlines, inset frames, bracket corners, and low-contrast technical texture. Make every edge belong to the same alignment system. Let the frame carry structure instead of adding heavy shadow.

Best for: product launches, technical narratives, catalogs, and concept presentations.

Avoid: decorative guides that do not align, mixed border weights, or random floating cards.

### Documentary ink

Use high-contrast black and warm white, billboard type, documentary media, and an irregular but narratively ordered collage. Let imagery and typography carry the character; keep ornament scarce.

Best for: agencies, architecture, production, art, and culture.

Avoid: fake projects or quotes, random brutalist misalignment, inaccessible contrast, and motion theater.

### Restrained dark glass

Use a near-black field, calm atmospheric depth, crisp edge highlights, and a small number of translucent shells. Keep the center breathable and use accent color only for status or focus.

Best for: premium technical products and cinematic launch pages where depth supports the story.

Avoid: colorful blob backgrounds, unreadable transparency, blur on every surface, and dense floating-card clutter.

## Build a distinct direction matrix

When exploring variants, hold the product truth constant and vary only a few art-direction dimensions:

| Dimension | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Story order | proof-first / mechanism-first / manifesto-first |
| Layout grammar | chapters / framed grid / split stage |
| Material | paper / ink / restrained glass |
| Type contrast | grotesk + serif / sans + mono / compressed + neutral |
| Signature moment | spotlight reveal / progress story / particle response / none |

Produce materially different directions, not color swaps. Once one is chosen, stop carrying unused systems into implementation.

## Handle media honestly

- Prefer supplied product media or commissioned/generated/licensed assets with recorded source and terms.
- Set width, height, aspect ratio, crop behavior, focal point, alt text, loading priority, and missing-media fallback deliberately.
- Do not present generated people as real staff, customers, or endorsers.
- Use real company logos only in truthful contexts; omit a logo wall when proof is unavailable.
- Simple authored marks, icons, diagrams, and data graphics are fine when they are clearly part of the new system rather than imitations of reference artwork.
- Keep a provenance note beside the project when external or generated media ships.

## Write for the page

- One hero message, one supporting thought, and one primary action are usually enough above the fold.
- Use concrete nouns and verbs; remove unearned superlatives.
- Give every chapter a distinct job and visible action where one exists.
- Keep labels concise, body measure readable, and line breaks intentional at each breakpoint.
- Do not create fictional outcomes to make a concept look complete. Label prototypes and illustrative data honestly.

## Originality check before handoff

Compare the result with every supplied reference across copy, brands, numbers, imagery, assets, structure, and motion. For each suspicious overlap, cite both locations and change the identity-bearing element—not merely its color. If reference access is incomplete, say what remains unverified rather than declaring the result clear.
